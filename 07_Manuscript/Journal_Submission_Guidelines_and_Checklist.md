## 3. Peer-Review Management & Rebuttal Strategy

When receiving peer-review feedback, use this structured approach to ensure rapid acceptance:

### Golden Rules for Response to Reviewers:
1. **Agree politely and constructively:** Even when reviewers misunderstand a point, thank them for highlighting an area where the manuscript's clarity can be improved.
2. **Point-by-point format:** Number every reviewer comment and provide an immediate, direct response followed by the exact revised text snippet and line numbers.
3. **Mark changes clearly:** Use tracked changes in Word or highlight revised text in yellow.

### Pre-Emptive Rebuttal Matrix for Common Reviewer Questions:

| Expected Reviewer Comment | Strategic Response & Defense |
| :--- | :--- |
| **"Why do so few studies (<15%) report multi-year field weathering data?"** | *"We thank the reviewer for this insightful observation. We completely agree. In fact, our review explicitly identifies this as a principal finding and systemic limitation of the continental literature (Section 4.4). Historically, African academic research has been constrained by short-term graduate degree timelines and equipment access, prioritizing 28-day laboratory compressive testing over multi-year outdoor weathering. We have expanded Section 4.4 and Section 5.2 to call for dedicated funding for long-term outdoor exposure test stations across African climatic zones."* |
| **"Why was the JBI Quasi-Experimental tool selected instead of Cochrane RoB?"** | *"We appreciate this methodological question. The Cochrane Risk of Bias tool is specifically engineered for randomized controlled clinical trials in medical healthcare. For experimental laboratory trials in civil engineering and materials science—where interventions consist of material substitutions and compaction pressures rather than human randomized cohorts—the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist for Quasi-Experimental Studies is the globally recognized standard. As documented in Section 2.7 and Table 5, this tool rigorously evaluates baseline material equivalence, control group presence, instrument calibration, and testing protocol consistency."* |
| **"Can the authors clarify why sandcrete blocks fail so frequently in African markets?"** | *"We thank the reviewer. In Section 1.3 and Section 4.2 ('The Sandcrete Paradox'), we have added granular economic and forensic detail: artisanal block molders operate in an informal, unregulated market where inflation incentivizes diluting cement ratios beyond 1:12 to 1:16 (compared to the recommended 1:6 or 1:8). Furthermore, lack of portable compressive testing equipment on job sites prevents private builders from verifying strength, leading to the widespread commercialization of blocks yielding 1.0–1.8 MPa despite national standards (NIS 87) mandating 2.5 MPa."* |
| **"How were language biases addressed in non-Anglophone African countries?"** | *"We thank the reviewer for raising this important contextual point. While inclusion was bounded by publications in English to ensure cross-database comparability per our registered protocol (Section 2.2), our search engines (AJOL, OpenAlex, Google Scholar) comprehensively indexed peer-reviewed works originating from Francophone and Lusophone African nations (e.g., Burkina Faso, Senegal, Cameroon, Mozambique, Algeria) that published empirical findings in English or English-abstracted international venues. We have explicitly noted this in Section 4.4 as a consideration for future multi-lingual reviews."* |
