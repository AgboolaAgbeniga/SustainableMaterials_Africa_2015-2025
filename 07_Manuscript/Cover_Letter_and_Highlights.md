**Dear Editor-in-Chief,**

I am pleased to submit our comprehensive systematic review entitled **"Sustainable Building Materials in Africa (2015–2025): A Systematic Evidence Synthesis of Material Classes, Technical Performance, and Implementation Contexts"** for consideration for publication as a Review Article in the *Journal of Building Engineering*.

Sub-Saharan Africa is undergoing rapid demographic and urban expansion, requiring an estimated 250 million new housing units by 2050. However, the continent's construction sector remains heavily reliant on carbon-intensive Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) concrete and artisanal sandcrete masonry, driving river sand depletion, consuming scarce foreign exchange reserves through clinker importation, and precipitating building failures due to substandard artisanal block production. Over the 2015–2025 decadal window, African and international researchers have produced extensive experimental literature on alternative materials—including compressed stabilized earth blocks (CSEBs), limestone calcined clay cement (LC3), agricultural pozzolans, and bio-composites. Nonetheless, this evidence base has remained fragmented across regional journals, institutional repositories, and siloed research domains without unified systematic synthesis.

To address this critical evidence gap, this study presents a rigorous decadal systematic review conducted in accordance with **PRISMA 2020** reporting guidelines and **Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI)** Critical Appraisal methodology. Synthesizing data across 9,112 bibliographic records identified from four primary databases (Google Scholar, OpenAlex, DOAJ, and AJOL), the final review comprehensively analyzes **2,492 verified primary empirical studies** across 31 standardized parameters, complemented by a multi-tool quality appraisal evaluating laboratory trials, socio-technical surveys, and Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs).

**Key findings of this review include:**
1. **Mechanical Viability:** Compressed stabilized earth blocks (3.5–9.2 MPa compressive strength) reliably deliver 2 to 3 times the loadbearing capacity of informal commercial sandcrete blocks (1.0–1.8 MPa) prevalent in informal urban markets, comfortably satisfying Nigerian NIS 87 and Ghanaian GS 297 standards (2.5–2.8 MPa).
2. **Structural Decarbonization:** LC3-50 and agricultural pozzolanic concretes (rice husk ash, palm oil fuel ash) achieve structural grades (22.0–52.0 MPa) while mitigating embodied carbon by 30% to 42% relative to conventional 100% OPC concrete (350 kg CO₂-eq/m³ baseline).
3. **Biogenic Carbon Storage:** Renewable structural bamboo demonstrates high axial tensile capacity (120–240 MPa) and significant biogenic carbon storage potential during culm maturation, although net life-cycle balances depend strictly on service-life preservation and circular end-of-life pathways.
4. **Methodological Transparency:** Quality appraisal reveals an evidence base characterized by high physical rigor but predominantly Moderate Risk of Bias (75.3%), driven by widespread under-reporting of statistical variance across replicate specimens (51.7% weighted compliance).
5. **Implementation Framework:** Synthesizing 137 dedicated adoption studies identifies institutional inertia (78.1%), supply chain fragmentation (71.5%), laboratory deficits (63.5%), and perceptual stigma (60.6%) as primary barriers, outlining actionable solutions including performance-based ARSO code gazetting (ARS 680–684) and green public procurement quotas.
