**Dear Editor-in-Chief,**

I am pleased to submit our comprehensive systematic review entitled **"Sustainable Building Materials in Africa (2015–2025): A Systematic Review"** for consideration for publication as a Full Review Article in *[Journal Name]*.

Sub-Saharan Africa is undergoing unprecedented demographic and urban expansion, requiring an estimated 250 million new housing units by 2050. However, the continent’s built environment remains locked into carbon-intensive Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) concrete and substandard commercial sandcrete masonry, driving catastrophic environmental degradation (such as uncurbed river sand mining) and frequent structural collapses. Over the 2015–2025 decadal window, African and international researchers have produced extensive experimental work on alternative materials—including compressed stabilized earth blocks (CSEBs), limestone calcined clay cement (LC3), agricultural pozzolans, and bio-composites. Nonetheless, this evidence base has remained critically fragmented across regional journals, institutional repositories, and siloed research domains.

To address this critical gap, this study presents the first exhaustive decadal systematic review conducted in strict accordance with **PRISMA 2020** guidelines and the **Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI)** Critical Appraisal methodology. Synthesizing data across 9,112 bibliographic records identified from four primary databases (Google Scholar, OpenAlex, DOAJ, and AJOL), the final review comprehensively analyzes **2,822 empirical laboratory and field studies** across 29 standardized parameters. 

**Key findings of this review include:**
1. **Structural Reliability:** Compressed stabilized earth blocks (3.5–9.2 MPa compressive strength) reliably deliver 2 to 3 times the load-bearing strength of commercial sandcrete blocks (1.0–1.8 MPa) prevalent in informal markets, while slashing cement consumption by 50–70%.
2. **Industrial Scalability:** LC3 and agricultural pozzolanic binders (rice husk ash, sugarcane bagasse ash) achieve full commercial structural grades (25.0–52.0 MPa) while mitigating embodied carbon by 18% to 42%.
3. **Environmental Carbon Sinks:** Renewable structural bamboo and agro-composites demonstrate net negative carbon footprints (-120 to -350 kg CO₂-eq/m³), presenting viable pathways for continental decarbonization.
4. **Implementation Framework:** An empirical barriers-versus-enablers matrix delineates actionable solutions for transitioning past outdated colonial prescriptive building codes, fragmented supply chains, and social perception stigmas.

This manuscript is an original work that has not been published previously and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All underlying screening logs, deduplication registers, extraction matrices, and JBI appraisal sheets are openly preserved and verifiable via the Open Science Framework ([https://osf.io/dvukp/](https://osf.io/dvukp/)) and Zenodo ([DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22771539](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22771539)).
